Understanding Trench Warfare

19th Century Tactics, 20th Century Technology

1915 to 1918

When WWI began both sides expected a short war of movement. By the winter of 1914 the armies were stuck, they dug in, and the line of trenches ran more than four hundred miles from the English Channel to Switzerland. Neither side could go around this line, so for three years they tried to go through it.

Generals still trained officers to win battles the way Napoleon won them: mass your men, charge the enemy line, break it. That tactic met machine guns, barbed wire and industrial artillery. The result was stalemate and roughly nine million dead soldiers.
