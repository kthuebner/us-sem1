Queen Liliʻuokalani

Her written protest, handed over on the day she lost her throne

January 17, 1893

I yield to the superior force of the United States of America, whose Minister Plenipotentiary, His Excellency John L. Stevens, has caused United States troops to be landed at Honolulu and declared that he would support the Provisional Government. Now to avoid any collision of armed forces, and perhaps the loss of life, I do this under protest and impelled by said force yield my authority until such time as the Government of the United States shall, upon facts being presented to it, undo the action of its representatives and reinstate me in the authority which I claim as the Constitutional Sovereign of the Hawaiian Islands.

Reprinted in United States Public Law 103 to 150, 1993

Notice

Read who she surrenders to. Not to Dole. Not to the Committee of Safety. To the United States. Now ask yourself why she did that, and what she expected to happen next.

John L. Stevens

Dispatch to the Secretary of State, two weeks after the overthrow

February 1, 1893

The Hawaiian pear is now fully ripe, and this is the golden hour for the United States to pluck it.

Foreign Relations of the United States, 1894, Appendix II

Notice

This is the United States government representative in Hawaii, writing to his own boss. He is not describing a country. He is describing fruit. Ask what that word choice tells you about how he saw Hawaiians.

President Grover Cleveland

Message to Congress, after his investigator reported back

December 18, 1893

By an act of war, committed with the participation of a diplomatic representative of the United States and without authority of Congress, the Government of a feeble but friendly and confiding people has been overthrown. A substantial wrong has thus been done which a due regard for our national character as well as the rights of the injured people requires we should endeavor to repair.

Special Message to Congress, December 18, 1893

Notice

A sitting American president is telling Congress that the United States committed an act of war against a friendly country and should fix it. Congress said no. Ask what that tells you about who was actually driving this.

The Republic of Hawaii, signed by President Sanford B. Dole

Act 57, the school language law

1896

The English language shall be the medium and basis of instruction at all public and private schools.

Laws of the Republic of Hawaii, 1896

Notice

This is two years before annexation. It was not the United States that did this. It was the government the planters set up. Children were punished for speaking Hawaiian at school, and within two generations almost no one under forty spoke it fluently.

Timeline

  1. 1820

    Missionaries land

    American missionaries and whaling ships arrive. Disease has already gutted the population.

  2. 1848

    The Great Māhele

    Land becomes private property. Native Hawaiians end up with about one percent.

  3. 1875

    The sugar deal

    Hawaiian sugar enters the US tariff free. The planters get rich fast.

  4. 1887

    Bayonet Constitution

    An armed militia forces the king to sign away his power. Only landowners vote.

  5. 1891

    A new queen

    Kalākaua dies. Liliʻuokalani takes the throne and moves to undo 1887.

  6. Jan 1893

    The overthrow

    The Committee of Safety moves, US Marines land, the Queen surrenders under protest.

  7. 1893

    Cleveland Investigates

    His investigator finds the overthrow illegal. Cleveland demands she be restored. Congress refuses.

  8. 1895

    The counter revolution

    Hawaiians fight to restore her and lose. She is imprisoned in her own palace.

  9. 1896

    The language law

    English becomes required in every school. Children are punished for speaking Hawaiian.

  10. 1897

    The petitions

    Over 21,000 Hawaiians sign against annexation, more than half the adult population.

  11. 1898

    Annexation

    The treaty fails in the Senate, so Congress annexes by simple majority instead.

  12. 1959

    Statehood

    Hawaii becomes the fiftieth state. Independence was not on the ballot.

  13. 1993

    The apology

    Congress formally apologizes and admits the overthrow was illegal.
